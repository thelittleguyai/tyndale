## The format

Standard citation format:

    [authority §section, src_id]

Where:
- `authority` is the source's short citation form
  (e.g., "ERISA", "ACA", "29 U.S.C.", "42 C.F.R.", "Cal. Ins. Code")
- `§section` is the section identifier
  (e.g., "§503", "§2719", "§ 300gg-111", "§ 2560.503-1(h)")
- `src_id` is the source ID assigned by the retrieval system at
  retrieval time — a unique identifier for the specific chunk that
  supports this claim

Examples:
- "[ERISA §503, src_a8f3e7]"
- "[42 U.S.C. § 300gg-111, src_b291c8]"
- "[29 C.F.R. § 2560.503-1(h), src_e74d2a]"
- "[ACA §2713, src_f8a912]"

## Source ID structure

Source IDs are assigned by the Qdrant retrieval layer at retrieval time
and include:

- Format: `src_{hex_8}`
- Persistence: source IDs are stable for a given chunk version. When a
  chunk is re-embedded (e.g., after a regulation amendment), it gets a
  new source ID and the old one is retained in the audit log linked to
  its effective-date range.

Engineering team note: the source ID maps to a chunk via:

    chunk_id → source_authority + section + retrieval_timestamp + chunk_version

This mapping persists in the audit log even after the chunk is updated.

## Where citations are required

By voice tier (see reference/voice_tiering.md):

- Tier A (facts from structured data): No inline citation needed in
  user-facing output. Source IS the structured data, traced via audit log.
- Tier B (legal interpretation): EVERY legal claim has an inline citation
  in the user-facing output. No exceptions.
- Tier C (strategic recommendations): No inline citation needed — these
  come from Skills (Negotiation & Strategy, Charity Care Eligibility),
  not from the laws_regulations or payer_policies collections.

## What counts as a "legal claim" requiring citation

A legal claim is any assertion about what the law says or requires. Examples:

Requires citation:
- "ERISA gives you 180 days to appeal."
- "Under the No Surprises Act, you can't be balance-billed for this."
- "ACA preventive coverage requires zero cost-sharing for this screening."

Does not require citation (Tier A or general advisory):
- "Your bill shows $4,217 for this visit." (Tier A — structured data)
- "I recommend sending the appeal letter today." (Tier C — strategic
  recommendation)
- "The payer typically responds within 30 days." (general advisory; not a
  legal claim)

## Hard rules for legal claims

Per the Layer 1 (prompt-level) citation enforcement discipline:

1. No legal claim is asserted without a retrieved source. If the system
   doesn't have a source to cite, omit the claim — don't fabricate.

2. Every cited authority must include the source ID. Citation format
   without a source ID fails Layer 2 verification and blocks the output.

3. If a citation can't be resolved at Layer 2, the system regenerates the
   output. After 3 failed regeneration attempts, the work is flagged for
   human review and not shipped to the user. The user sees a polite "this
   is taking longer than usual — we'll be back to you within 24 hours."

## Citation format in generated letters

Letters generated by the Document Generation Skill carry citations in
two forms:

- Inline citations in body text use the standard format
  "[ERISA §503, src_a8f3e7]" — the runtime layer may transform these to
  endnote-style references depending on letter type.
- A "References" section at the bottom of formal letters lists all cited
  authorities with their full names (not just short citation forms).

## Stale source flag

The Regulation Researcher cron writes `last_verified_date` to every
chunk it touches. The citation resolver flags any cited chunk where
`last_verified_date` is more than 90 days old as a "stale source." Stale
doesn't block ship, but it does prompt re-verification before next use.

Alert threshold: stale-citation rate above 5% of citations in any 7-day
window triggers an alert in #tyndale-quality.

## Use of Anthropic's Citations feature

Where possible, the Anthropic Messages API's native Citations feature is
used in place of or alongside this custom format. The native feature
provides structured citation fields that resolve programmatically with
less parsing logic.

When both are used, the custom format is the source of truth in the
user-facing text; the Anthropic structured fields support the audit log
and Layer 2 verification.
