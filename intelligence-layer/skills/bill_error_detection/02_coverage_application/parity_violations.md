# Mental health / SUD parity violations

**What this is.** Mental health or substance-use-disorder (MH/SUD) benefits are subjected
to more restrictive financial requirements or treatment limitations (including
non-quantitative treatment limitations, NQTLs) than medical/surgical benefits.

**Detection signals.**
- Higher copay/coinsurance or a separate deductible for MH/SUD than for medical/surgical.
- Stricter visit limits, or NQTLs (prior auth, medical-necessity criteria, network
  admission) applied more stringently to MH/SUD.

**Citation language.** Tier B: "The MH/SUD financial requirement / treatment limitation is
more restrictive than the predominant medical-surgical standard, violating mental-health
parity [MHPAEA, 29 U.S.C. §1185a, src_TBD]."

**Severity.** Medium; strong legal basis but fact-intensive (requires comparison).

**Common defenses.** Insurer asserts comparability. Response: request the plan's
comparative analysis of the NQTL.

**Required evidence.** SBC, EOB, the MH/SUD benefit terms, and the medical/surgical
comparison.

**Recommended remediation (Tier C).** Appeal citing parity; request the NQTL comparative
analysis. Sequence via `negotiation_strategy`.
